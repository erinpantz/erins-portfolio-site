<Section heading="From BookMD to Vim">

Vim started out as BookMD, a company that helped patients book imaging appointments faster while saving money on out-of-pocket costs.

I came on as the first designer and fourth team member. Over the four years I was there, we launched products for patients and providers, rebranded, grew the team significantly, and raised two rounds of funding. I built Vim’s brand system: its name, its logo, its illustration style, and how it showed up in the product, campaigns, sales story, and our offices.

</Section>

<Container><Divider /></Container>

<Section heading="A new name">

By 2017, the company was looking beyond appointment booking, and the name “BookMD” was too narrow. So we started on a journey to find a new name with:

<UnorderedList>
  <UnorderedListItem title="Flexibility">Our name shouldn’t pigeonhole us.</UnorderedListItem>
  <UnorderedListItem title="Simplicity">It should be easy to say and easy to remember.</UnorderedListItem>
  <UnorderedListItem title="Originality">We needed to stand out in a fast-growing market of health tech companies.</UnorderedListItem>
</UnorderedList>

I pitched “Vim,” as in “vim and vigor.” The concept was flexible, the word was short and easy to remember, and it had a unique quality, since it’s not often used today. It represented the energy of our team and the good health we wished for our customers.

</Section>

<Container><Divider /></Container>

<Section heading="A new logo">

Now that we had a name befitting Vim’s ambitions, we needed a logo to match. I started exploring the “v” heart and how its shapes could extend to the other letters in the word.

I used simple shapes to show how they come together to make a whole, much as Vim connects systems to give customers a seamless experience. Once I had the form, I thickened the shapes so the word would take up as much space as possible, so it reads well even when it’s small.

<Section heading="The brand in the product">

Our patient and provider tools were where most people met the brand, so the brand had to work there first. Three principles guided how it showed up in the product:

<UnorderedList>
  <UnorderedListItem title="Mobile first">
    Our patient and provider tools were web apps. I knew responsive design well from my years in ecommerce, so I took a mobile-first approach to make sure the apps worked well on every device.
  </UnorderedListItem>
  <UnorderedListItem title="Accessible">
    I had learned accessibility best practices from a product leader at PayPal, and applied them in this role. That came in handy when insurers were vetting our app.
  </UnorderedListItem>
  <UnorderedListItem title="Joyful">
    Our Head of Product, Yael, was a wonderful partner who always considered our users’ emotional state and motivation. Together we gave the experience humanity and joy, with friendly copy and the illustrations carried into loading and sign-in screens.
  </UnorderedListItem>
</UnorderedList>

<Section heading="Campaigns">

Our first commercial partner was Disney, a self-funded employer who wanted employees healthy while saving on the cost of care.

### Mammogram campaign

Disney asked us to raise the number of employees getting preventive mammograms with a targeted campaign. They provided a list of eligible employees, and we handled outreach, booking, rewards, and reporting.

Yael and I wrote the slogan “Take Care of You,” and I designed its type treatment, the digital experience, and tote bags.

The brand system made room for partners: we brought the health system’s colors and logo into the whole experience.
